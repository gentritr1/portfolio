# Creative consult: Gentrit Rashiti portfolio

Date: 2026-10-04. Reviewer: creative director (interactive). Inputs: CONTENT.md, DRAFTS.md, the two Astra briefs, JURY.md, the 16 desktop + 4 mobile captures, the earlier boards, and the reference sites (fetched as text where the host allowed it; the rest from direct knowledge of those sites).

Short verdict first. The 16 drafts solved "pale". They did not solve "creative". The energy fix created a new skeleton: a flood colour, a giant grotesk name top-left, a screenshot on the right, and one gimmick. Twelve of sixteen drafts sit on that skeleton. The owner's older complaint ("themes painted on one skeleton") is still true, now in colour. Creativity comes from a rule the whole site obeys, not from a theme. That is the thread through every section below.

### 1.2 The slop checklist (markers of generic "AI portfolio")

A draft with three or more of these is generic under the skin, whatever the colour.

- Giant first-and-last name top-left, grey one-liner under it. A trailing full stop on the name ("Rashiti.").
- "Frontend & mobile developer, now full stack" as the only story on screen.
- Gradient blobs, metaballs or "liquid" 3D with no meaning.
- Glass cards, bento grids, noise overlays, grain.
- Dark mode plus one accent. Fade-up on every section. Lenis smooth scroll plus reveal.
- Device mockups in a fan. An exploded laptop. A phone that tilts with the pointer.
- Fake terminal, fake boot log, fake HUD, stickers, stamps, "v2.0" captions.
- Retro TV, retro OS, retro game, 3D desk, 3D room: used as a costume (fails the M2 test).
- Split-flap or scramble text on hover. A cursor-following blob. Marquee of logos.
- "Selected work 01 02 03" rows with arrows and nothing else.
- Every draft on the same typeface.

### 1.3 The three references the owner gave, reduced to their rule

- designeer.xyz: M1 (dense aligned rows), M3 (a real dithered orb as the only picture), M11 (⌘K, keyboard first). Its calm comes from density, not from grey.
- offgrid.inc: M1 only. The work is the energy. The identity is a footnote.
- mek.gallery: M1 (pixel grid), M4 (one obsession), M6 (pixel and blackletter type), M10 (`.txt` voice). The cream paper is a result of the rule, not the rule.

Agreement with the self-scores: no. The self-scores sit in a 0.24 band (7.95–8.19). That is a scoring instrument that cannot say no. My spread is 5.5–7.5 and none reaches 8. Compressed scores are a sign the reviewer scored the feature list, not the capture, which the brief itself warned against.

Three structural facts the table hides:

1. Twelve drafts use one typeface (the Archivo-style grotesk) at one size on one layout. Type is identity. One typeface across 16 "directions" means there is one direction.
2. Seven drafts are costumes (studio, blueprint, orbit, desktop, primetime, desk, canvas). Remove the costume and a list remains.
3. The best moments are the small live recreations: the FJALË tiles, the care sparkline, the Riso print button. They are specific (M9). They are buried under hero names.

Keep for parts: issue (type), swiss (letter tiles), riso (print artefact), savefile (all-28 grid), wall (band composition), dither (shader). Do not repair the rest. Repair rounds on a costume produce a better costume.

## 3. New directions

Each one obeys one rule. Each one is built from the owner's own material. None of them starts with a giant name.

Shared constraints: facts only from CONTENT.md, public screenshots only, sound off by default, reduced-motion path, lazy chunk ≤150 kB gzip, works at 375 px, a recruiter reaches a project in 5 seconds.

### 3.1 DIFF — the migration is the site

- Core idea: the site shows two versions of itself, legacy and new, with one seam between them; the visitor drags the seam and watches the portfolio migrate route by route with parity rows turning green.
- Why not generic: M4 (his real obsession: route-by-route rewrites with parity tests) + M5 (the drag reveals what he does) + M9 (31 ADRs, 16→2 queries, 34 routes are real numbers).
- First viewport: a vertical seam at 38 %. Left: "v1", a flat 2021-era list in Nuxt-green on grey, slightly blurred. Right: "v2", the same content rebuilt, sharp, in signal green on bone. A parity strip along the bottom edge: 28 rows, `route · legacy ✓ · react ✓`, ticking as the seam passes each row. No hero name. The name is a row.
- Type: Hubot Sans (display, wide, GitHub, free) + Mona Sans (body) + JetBrains Mono (parity rows).
- Colour: bone `#F3EFE6` ground, signal green `#16C172`, legacy side `#2F3B2F` on `#DADBD3`, failing red `#E5484D` used once.
- Signature interaction: drag the seam (pointer or arrow keys). Each project row flips from v1 to v2 as the seam crosses it, with a View Transition per row. Press `P` to run "parity": the rows execute in sequence and the seam moves on its own.
- Signature animation: the seam is a `clip-path: inset()` driven by a scroll-timeline on mobile and by pointer on desktop (`animation-timeline: scroll()`, zero JS in the fallback). Row flip is a 3D `rotateX` with a real backface. The parity ticks use a `steps()` timing so they feel like a test runner.
- Recruiter in 5 s: the strip says "28 routes migrated, parity-tested" before anything moves. Click any row → case.
- Band: professional. Risk: low. No WebGL needed.

### 3.2 FJALËKRYQ — the crossword

- Core idea: the site map is an Albanian-style crossword. The project names cross each other in the grid; the clues are the one-line facts; a click on a word opens the project.
- Why not generic: M2 (the puzzle is the navigation) + M9 (Albanian, the Ë, his own FJALË dictionary) + M6 (letter tiles are the image).
- First viewport: a full-bleed grid of tiles on a hot mustard field. Filled in by default: BAYYINAH crosses READTOFEED at the A, VIVAFRESH drops through the V, FJALË sits at the centre with its Ë. Clues run down the right margin as a numbered list ("6 across: a video-learning platform, rebuilt, EN/AR"). A small toggle, "Solve it yourself", empties the grid.
- Type: Libre Franklin (tiles, caps, tight) + Fraunces (clues, soft serif) + Martian Mono (numbers).
- Colour: mustard `#F2B705` field, tile ink `#161616`, filled tiles `#FFFCF2`, the active word floods `#1F4BFF`.
- Signature interaction: typing. The visitor types a project name anywhere; the letters fall into the grid as physics bodies and settle into their squares. Arrow keys move the cursor between words. The current word's clue is read aloud by the layout, not by audio: it grows to display size in the margin.
- Signature animation: letters as 2D rigid bodies (hand-rolled verlet, 3 kB, no library) dropping into cells with a two-bounce settle. On word complete, the word's tiles flip to the project colour in a wave and the clue morphs into the case title with a View Transition.
- Recruiter in 5 s: the grid is pre-filled and the clues are the project list. Hover any word = its one-liner. Enter = case.
- Band: fun, with a professional reading. Risk: low to medium. Mobile: the grid is pinch-zoomable; the clue list is the fallback nav.

### 3.3 LINJA — the flip-dot board

- Core idea: the whole site is one electromechanical flip-disc display, the kind on a city bus. Every image, every word, is discs flipping, with the sound of it.
- Why not generic: M3 (a real instanced-disc renderer, not a filter) + M7 + M8 (timing and sound are the identity) + M9 (Morse Trainer timing drives the rhythm).
- First viewport: a 144×48 disc board filling the viewport, safety yellow on matte black. It boots: a wipe of discs flips to spell the destination line "GENTRIT RASHITI → WEB · MOBILE · FULL STACK", then holds, then flips the next "line" (a project) every 6 s. The small HTML text under the board is the only non-disc element: a route list, left-aligned, like a bus timetable.
- Type: no font on the board; the matrix glyphs are drawn in shader from a 5×7 / 7×9 bitmap set. HTML text: Archivo (already licensed) at small sizes.
- Colour: disc front `#F5C400`, disc back `#0E0E0E`, board `#121212`, one accent per project on the HTML side.
- Signature interaction: a key. Hold the mouse button or space anywhere: a short press is a dit, a long press a dah. The board decodes Morse at Farnsworth timing. "W" for work, "C" for contact, "?" shows the key. The route list also works with ordinary clicks.
- Signature animation: OGL instanced quads (6,912 discs), per-disc spring on a rotation attribute, wave-stagger from a seed point. Screenshots are quantised to the board (luma threshold with Bayer 4×4) and flip in; a click on a project then slides the real screenshot over the board as an HTML image. Sound: WebAudio, one short noise burst per disc with random pitch, voices capped at 64, off until the visitor turns it on.
- Recruiter in 5 s: the timetable list under the board is plain HTML with project, year, platform. The board is the picture.
- Band: crafted / experimental. Risk: medium. OGL ≈ 25 kB gzip; shader and bitmaps ≈ 8 kB. Reduced motion: the board renders the final frame, no flips, no sound.

### 3.4 FACING PAGES — the bilingual reader

- Core idea: the portfolio is a book, read in an EPUB reader he built; the left page is English, the right page mirrors the layout right-to-left in Arabic script direction.
- Why not generic: M2 (page turn, font size and progress are the navigation) + M9 (EN/AR RTL, Read to Feed's reader, Bayyinah's scripture reader) + M3 (the reflow is real text reflow).
- First viewport: the cover. A full-bleed cloth cover in oxblood with the title stamped in cream, a small spine label with "2021–2026". No nav. A thumb edge on the right shows the chapter tabs: Healthcare, Streaming, Reading, Web3, Games. Click or swipe opens to the first spread.
- Type: Literata (body, variable, made for e-readers) + Amiri (Arabic page) + Martian Mono (margin facts).
- Colour: cover `#7A1F2B`, paper `#FBF6EA`, ink `#1B1B1B`, chapter tabs in project colours.
- Signature interaction: the A- / A+ stepper changes the type size of the whole site and the book reflows and re-paginates live; the progress bar shows "page 7 of 31". A "⇄" control flips the whole spread to RTL and the English becomes the right page. Chapter tabs jump.
- Signature animation: a WebGL page curl (cylinder bend on a plane, back face holds the next page texture) at 500 ms; the DOM pages swap under it. Reflow uses CSS columns with `column-fill: auto` and a measured page count. On low power or reduced motion the curl is a plain cross-fade.
- Recruiter in 5 s: the cover's thumb tabs are the project list. The first spread is the "contents" page with 28 lines.
- Band: crafted. Risk: medium. Page texture capture is the hard part: snapshot each page to canvas via SVG `foreignObject` (same-origin fonts only), keep two textures live.

### 3.5 BITRATE — the stream is the site

- Core idea: the portfolio is an HLS player; the quality selector sets the rendering quality of the whole page, and the scrubber is his 2021–2026 timeline.
- Why not generic: M5 (the quality menu reveals how streaming works by doing it to the page) + M7 (the scrubber is time) + M9 (live chat, moderation, paywall are his real features).
- First viewport: a 16:9 player edge to edge. The "video" is a live canvas reel of public screenshots cut at 2 s with a soft field of the project's colour. A LIVE pill, a viewer count that ticks, a chat column where the facts arrive as messages every 2 s ("mod: 34 routes, 270+ components"). Below the player: the scrubber, 2021 to 2026, with chapter markers for each project.
- Type: Bricolage Grotesque (display, optical sizes) + IBM Plex Mono (overlay, chat).
- Colour: player black `#0A0A0A` but the colour field fills the whole frame, so the first viewport is never dark; chat on `#F4F4F0`.
- Signature interaction: the quality menu. Auto / 1080p / 720p / 480p / 240p. At 480p the entire page pixelates: images switch to 1/4 sources with `image-rendering: pixelated`, an SVG `feComponentTransfer` posterises colours to 4 bits, type snaps to a coarser size scale. At 240p the chat goes to one line. The scrubber scrubs the career: drag to 2023 and the player shows Viva Fresh and the chat shows that year's facts.
- Signature animation: the reel is canvas 2D with a hard cut every 2 s and a 120 ms colour bleed (M7: the cut is the beat). Quality change is a 300 ms "buffering" ring then an instant switch, like a real ABR ladder. Premium lock toggles the paywall card with a sharp 200 ms rise.
- Recruiter in 5 s: the chapter markers on the scrubber are labelled; the chat's pinned message is the one-liner; "Open project" sits under the player.
- Band: crafted. Risk: low to medium. No WebGL.

### 3.6 AISLE — the shelf, the scanner, the receipt

- Core idea: the work is stocked on a supermarket shelf; the visitor scans what they want; the basket prints a receipt that is the CV.
- Why not generic: M2 (scan to shortlist is a real function for a recruiter) + M8 (beep, thermal printer) + M9 (Viva Fresh, the Read to Feed barcode scanner).
- First viewport: a flat-lit shelf, three rows, each project a boxed product with its screenshot as the label and a red price tag that shows its one fact ("34 routes" / "RN 0.63→0.81"). Big shelf-talker type across the top: "AISLE 7 · WEB · MOBILE · FULL STACK". A basket count bottom right.
- Type: Anton (shelf talkers, price tags) + Public Sans (labels) + Courier Prime (receipt).
- Colour: shelf yellow `#FFD400`, price red `#E3001B`, box white, receipt paper `#FAFAF5`.
- Signature interaction: the pointer is a scanner. Hover a box and a red laser line sweeps it; click and it beeps (off by default), the price tag flips to "in basket". "Print receipt" opens a thermal receipt in a drawer: the chosen projects, dated, with his contact at the bottom, and a real print stylesheet that prints at 80 mm width. The receipt is also the CV download.
- Signature animation: boxes are CSS 3D (three faces, no WebGL); the shelf is a horizontal scroll-snap row per aisle. The receipt prints line by line with `steps()` and a slight paper-feed jitter.
- Recruiter in 5 s: the shelf is the project list with facts on the tags. Scan three, print, done.
- Band: fun. Risk: low. Mobile: one aisle per screen, swipe.

### 3.7 DEAL — the card table

- Core idea: projects are cards on a felt table with real weight; the visitor is dealt a hand; drag, flip and throw them.
- Why not generic: M8 (physics first, with mass and friction) + M9 (Za!, his server-authoritative card game) + M5 (the back of each card is the fact sheet).
- First viewport: a top-down felt table in tomato red, a face-down deck top left, a dealt hand of five cards fanning out in the first second. Each card face is a project; the suit is its channel (CH01–CH06 become six suits). No hero name: the dealer's name is on the chip rack.
- Type: Fraunces (card faces, soft italic numerals) + Archivo Narrow (card backs, facts).
- Colour: felt `#C8102E`, card `#FFFDF7`, suit inks: cobalt, forest, black, mustard, violet, coral.
- Signature interaction: drag to throw; cards slide with friction and stop. Double-click flips. "Deal again" deals a different five. "Show all" fans all 28 in an arc. A card thrown off the table respawns in the deck.
- Signature animation: 2D rigid bodies with hand-rolled verlet integration and simple box collision (cards do not need rotation collision; use circles for speed). Flip is a CSS 3D rotate with a real backface. Dealing uses a 60 ms stagger with an overshoot curve.
- Recruiter in 5 s: "Show all" is a labelled arc of 28 cards; each card face carries the project name and year.
- Band: fun. Risk: medium (touch and drag conflicts; must not block scroll).

### 3.8 LEDGER — every row is alive

- Core idea: one dense, aligned ledger of 28 rows, designeer-dense, where the right third of every row runs a tiny live recreation of that project.
- Why not generic: M1 (density as the only rule) + M5 (the row explains itself by running) + M11 (⌘K, keyboard, 60 fps across 28 live cells).

Columns: index, name, year, platform, one fact, and a 160×64 live cell: a sparkline ticking for Healthcare, a chat line arriving for Streaming, a page flipping for Reading, a QR flipping for Web3, five letter tiles filling for FJALË, a dot pulsing Morse for Morse Trainer. - Band: professional. Risk: low to medium (the perf discipline is the work).

## 4. Unreached 3D and motion moves

Each item: effect, technique, budget. Shared rules: lazy chunk ≤150 kB gzip (OGL ≈ 25 kB; three.js core is ≈ 150 kB gzip on its own, so prefer OGL or a hand-built renderer), 60 fps on a 2022 mid-range Android, `prefers-reduced-motion` gives a static final frame, every scene pauses when hidden or offscreen.

| # | Effect | Technique | Budget and fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The screenshot lights the scene. The phone's screen casts its own colours onto the desk and the wall. | Downscale the screenshot to an 8×8 mip on the CPU once; feed it as a tiny env texture; in the fragment shader sample it by surface normal as an area light and add it to the diffuse term. | +2 kB. Static if reduced motion. Fixes the dead "desk" look without shadow maps. |
| 2 | Real UI rendered as a texture in 3D, interactive. | Do not rasterise the DOM. Place the real DOM in 3D with `matrix3d` from the camera's projection (CSS3D method) and render only the environment in WebGL under it. Screens stay crisp, clickable and accessible. | +4 kB. Fallback: flat DOM. |
| 3 | Flip-disc board (3.3). | OGL instanced quads; attributes: rotation, target, spring velocity; wave stagger by distance from a seed; discs are two-sided with a yellow front and a black back. | ≈ 33 kB with OGL. 7k instances in one draw call. Reduced motion: final frame only. |
| 4 | Granular mechanical sound for flips, scans, prints. | WebAudio: one 20 ms noise burst through a band-pass with random centre, scheduled with `AudioContext.currentTime`; voice cap 64; master gain ramps in over 200 ms. | 0 kB. Off until the visitor opts in; the toggle persists. |
| 5 | Morse timing as the site's clock. | One `dit` constant (60 ms at 20 wpm Farnsworth). Transition durations are integer multiples of it; the key uses a 600 Hz oscillator with a 5 ms attack. | 0 kB. Reduced motion keeps the timing but removes travel. |
| 6 | Letters as physics bodies (3.2). | Hand-rolled verlet: position, previous position, radius; cells are static boxes; 2 sub-steps at 60 Hz; sleep when velocity < 0.01. | ≈ 3 kB. No matter.js (25 kB gzip) and never Rapier (wasm ≈ 500 kB). |
| 7 | One project name melts into the next. | MSDF text: generate one atlas at build time (msdf-bmfont), draw each name as an SDF; morph by lerping the two distance fields and thresholding at 0.5. | Atlas ≈ 60 kB PNG once; shader 2 kB. Fallback: cross-fade. |
| 8 | Page curl (3.4). | Plane with 64 segments; vertex shader bends around a moving cylinder axis; `gl_FrontFacing` selects the front or back texture. | ≈ 30 kB with OGL. Two textures live. Fallback: cross-fade. |
| 9 | Quality-ladder rendering of the whole page (3.5). | `srcset` steps with `image-rendering: pixelated`; an SVG filter on the root (`feComponentTransfer` with `type="discrete"` tables) for posterising; font-size snaps to a coarser scale. | 0 kB. Filters on the root are costly on mobile: apply the filter to the player frame only below 768 px. |
